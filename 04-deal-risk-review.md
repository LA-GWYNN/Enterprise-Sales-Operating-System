# Deal Risk Review

**Purpose:** A weekly inspection of open deals that separates what is *known* from what is *hoped*, assigns every risk a next action, and keeps the forecast honest.

**Cadence:** Weekly for deals in the current-quarter forecast; biweekly for early pipeline. Takes about 5-10 minutes per deal.

**Core rule:** A deal stays in a forecast category only if its evidence supports it. Good feelings do not count.

## 4. Forecast category rules (starting hypotheses, calibrate to your history)

| Category | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| **Pipeline** | Pain confirmed; business owner named |
| **Best case** | Mutual action plan exists; Economic Buyer path identified; no High risks without a mitigation |
| **Commit** | Economic Buyer engaged; paper process underway with dates; close date buyer-confirmed; no High risks on Budget, Paper, or Champion |

**Downgrade triggers:**
- Close date moves without a buyer reason
- Champion goes quiet for more than two weeks
- New stakeholder appears with no prior engagement
- Security or legal not started inside 60 days of close
- Buyer cancels or postpones a milestone in the mutual action plan

---

## 5. Questions a skeptical sales leader would ask

1. Who on the buyer's side has said, in writing or on a call, that this will happen by the close date?
2. What is the single biggest reason this deal could slip, and what are you doing about it this week?
3. If your Champion left tomorrow, what would happen?
4. What does the buyer do if they do not buy?
5. What do you know about the competition that is not in the CRM?
6. Which of your statements here are fact, and which are assumption?

---

## 6. Recovery playbook for a stalled deal

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1. Diagnose | Which scorecard elements dropped, or were never confirmed? |
| 2. Reset the conversation | Ask directly: "What has changed since we last spoke?" |
| 3. Re-anchor to pain | Return to the original trigger and the cost of waiting |
| 4. Widen the thread | Add a second contact, ideally the business owner or Economic Buyer |
| 5. Offer a smaller step | A scoped pilot or a narrower use case, only if it still serves the buyer's outcome |
| 6. Set a decision point | Agree on a date to proceed or close the opportunity |
| 7. Close out cleanly | If there is no path, disqualify and record why |
